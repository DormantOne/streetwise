Five-Year Strategic & Sustainability Plan · 2026–2031
The Vision

The nation's leading academic center for street medicine.

Excellence across clinical care, education, research, and advocacy — measured by one thing: better health for people experiencing homelessness.

Financial Sustainability

Grants are a launchpad — not life support.

No single source carries the Center. Six independent streams converge into one stable base — each doing a distinct job.

$1.25M+ annual baseline before any clinical revenue

University support funds core operations and leadership. The HRSA grant funds residency training and workforce development. The Cooper Fund and UPMC Shadyside Foundation power outreach and the inpatient consult service. County opioid-settlement dollars fund integrated addiction care — a pharmacist, a social worker, and a community health worker.

How the Care Pays

A stitch in time — and a bill at the point of care.

One — low-cost street clinics, social work, and psychiatric care keep people well, and out of the ED and the inpatient bed. The consult service proves it: fewer readmissions, shorter stays, less emergency use — the costs the health system already carries.

Two — the care itself is reimbursable. Place of Service 27 makes street encounters professionally billable: visits, chronic disease, behavioral health, substance-use treatment, and care coordination.

The Model

A flywheel, not a budget.

Clinical revenue is reinvested into physicians, interdisciplinary staff, research, and outreach — which expands billable encounters, strengthens grant competitiveness, and generates more revenue. Sustainability compounds.
